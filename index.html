    Working

    

          
    
            What this assumes
            
      
              
    • 
                Everything is rigid and the pole is fixed solidly to the plate — no bending,
                no flexing, no wobbling in its socket.
              
      • 
              
    • 
                The ground is flat, level and hard. A plate sinking into soft ground or
                resting on a slope behaves differently.
              
      • 
              
    • 
                The push is steady and slow. A sharp knock can tip something that a steady
                push of the same size would not, which is what the energy figure is for.
              
      • 
              
    • 
                The baseplate is a uniform slab and the pole a uniform rod, both with their
                weight evenly spread, and the pole is centred on the plate.
              
      • 
              
    • 
                The force is the level at which it starts to lift. That is also the
                hardest part — once it is past the balance point it keeps going on its own,
                and needs less and less force on the way over.
              
      • 
              
    • 
                It answers "will it tip", not "will it break". A tall pole may bend or shear
                at its base well before the plate lifts.
